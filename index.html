Research

Aerial swarms

How can many drones fly fast through cluttered, unknown space without a central planner? Each drone runs model predictive control over time-aware safe corridors: convex regions of free space that keep it clear of obstacles, of space it has not yet seen, and of its neighbours.

Flapping-wing flight

Bird-scale flapping-wing drones are quiet and safe to fly near people and wildlife, but their lift, airspeed and turning are coupled through a few periodic inputs. Models of these couplings that are compact enough to optimise over in real time let these aircraft track agile trajectories accurately.

Highlighted papers

  • Voxel-grid based convex decomposition of 3D space for safe corridor generation

    C. Toumieh, A. Lambert

    Journal of Intelligent & Robotic Systems, 2022

    Splits the free space of a voxel grid into overlapping convex polyhedra, which turn collision avoidance into linear constraints.

  • Decentralized multi-agent planning using model predictive control and time-aware safe corridors

    C. Toumieh, A. Lambert

    IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, 2022

    Time-aware safe corridors encode where the other agents will be as constraints in a real-time, decentralised MPC planner.

  • High-speed motion planning for aerial swarms in unknown and cluttered environments

    C. Toumieh, D. Floreano

    IEEE Transactions on Robotics, 2024

    A decentralised planner that lets every drone in a swarm fly fast while staying clear of the others and of unexplored space.

  • Accurate trajectory tracking with model predictive contouring control for bird-scale flapping-wing MAVs

    C. Toumieh, J. Zeng, N. Mistry, D. Floreano

    IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, 2026

    A nine-state model identified from flight data enables, to our knowledge, the first contouring control of bird-scale flapping flight.

  • SwarmNxt: open-source software–hardware platform for fast and agile aerial swarms

    C. Toumieh, N. Mistry, B. Jarvis, S. Jeger, P. Liu, S. Shen, D. Floreano

    IEEE Int. Symp. Safety, Security, and Rescue Robotics (SSRR), 2026, accepted

    An open platform for real swarms: assembly guide, parallel software deployment, and one ROS 2 stack from perception to control.
